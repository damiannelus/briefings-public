## Macro Spotlight – Deep Dive  
**AI and Intellectual Property: Navigating Legal and Ethical Challenges in Drug Discovery**  

The legal framework for AI-generated inventions is currently inadequate, as evidenced by the case of Insilico Medicine and the denial of inventor status to AI systems like DABUS by US courts. These legal rulings underscore a critical gap in intellectual property laws, which are not yet equipped to handle the complexities of AI-driven innovation. This issue is not merely academic—it has real-world implications for companies and researchers relying on AI to accelerate drug discovery and other scientific advancements.  

Current laws require human inventors to be named on patents, even when AI plays a central role in the discovery process. This creates a paradox where AI is the primary driver of innovation, yet it is excluded from legal recognition. Legal experts argue that this may hinder future innovation, as the exclusion of AI from patent protection could discourage investment in AI-driven research. The US Patent and Trademark Office has taken a firm stance that AI is merely a tool, like a calculator, and does not need to be named as an inventor. However, this perspective is increasingly being challenged as AI systems become more autonomous and capable of generating novel ideas.  

For technical product managers, AI product owners, delivery leads, and engineering managers transitioning to AI-native development, this legal uncertainty presents both a challenge and an opportunity. It highlights the need for clear guidelines on how to attribute credit and responsibility in AI-driven workflows. Teams working on advanced LLM applications and agentic workflows must consider the legal implications of their AI systems, particularly in domains like drug discovery, where intellectual property is a key concern. This also raises questions about how to structure AI governance frameworks that align with current legal standards while preparing for future changes.  

The debate over AI inventor status is not limited to the pharmaceutical industry—it has broader implications for intellectual property laws in general. As AI becomes more integrated into the innovation process, the legal system will need to evolve to reflect this reality. For AI-native product leaders, this means staying ahead of legal trends and ensuring that their teams are prepared to navigate the evolving landscape of AI governance and compliance.
