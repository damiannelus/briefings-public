**Strategic Thread: The Evolving Role of AI in Software Extensibility and Security**  

The intersection of LLMs and modern sandboxing technologies is creating new opportunities for extensible software on the web, as outlined in Jeremy Morrell’s hypothesis. This vision hinges on the ability to securely execute user-submitted code, which is where tools like smolvm 1.8.3 come into play. smolvm provides a secure, hardware-isolated environment for running untrusted Python and JavaScript, with cold starts taking 0.6–1.5 seconds and warm executions as fast as 50 ms. This performance is critical for real-world applications such as data transformations, where speed and security are paramount.  

The implications for Hands-on AI engineers are clear: secure execution environments are becoming essential for building extensible applications. smolvm’s ability to enforce CPU/RAM limits, no-network execution, and storage quotas ensures that malicious code or infinite loops are mitigated, making it a viable option for production workloads. This aligns with Jeremy Morrell’s vision of a future where users can safely extend applications with AI-generated functionality, but only if the underlying infrastructure is robust and secure.  

At the same time, the limitations of CoT explanations in audit and compliance contexts, as highlighted by the Google DeepMind paper, underscore the need for more reliable auditing mechanisms. CoT traces are not faithful representations of actual model behavior, with contradiction rates as high as 13.49% in some models. This is a major concern for production AI systems, especially those involving agentic decision-making over extended interactions. Engineers must now consider network-level records as the gold standard for auditing, rather than relying on CoT explanations.  

This dual focus on secure execution and auditing highlights the growing complexity of deploying AI in production. As models become more capable through post-training scaling laws and RL on complex environments, the infrastructure and tooling required to manage them must also evolve. This includes not only secure sandboxes but also robust observability and reliability frameworks that can handle the nuances of AI-driven systems.  

For forward-deployed engineers, the takeaway is clear: the next wave of AI engineering will be defined by the integration of secure execution environments, reliable auditing mechanisms, and the ability to scale AI capabilities without compromising on security or compliance.
